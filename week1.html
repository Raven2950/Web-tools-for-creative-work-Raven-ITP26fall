Creative Process — Interactive / Physical Computing Projects

Notes gathered from interview with Jerome

A Short Version

Idea → Research → Interaction Sketch → Quick Prototype → Test → Iterate → Refine Experience → Document

1. Starting Point
  • Jerome usually starts with an interaction, behavior, or experience rather than a specific technology.
  • He thinks about what he wants the user to feel, notice, or do .
  • Sometimes the idea comes from a physical gesture, sound, object, or an everyday interaction that interests him.
  • He tries to describe the idea in one simple sentence before developing it further.
2. Research & References
  • Jerome looks at existing projects, products, artworks, and interaction examples.
  • He collects visual and technical references.
  • He thinks about what already exists and what could be explored differently.
  • He also checks what technologies or materials might make the idea possible.
3. Sketching the Interaction
  • Before building, Jerome sketches the basic interaction flow.
  • He asks:
  • What does the user do?
  • What does the system sense?
  • How does the system respond?
  • He often uses simple drawings, diagrams, or notes instead of making detailed designs at this stage.
4. Quick Prototyping
  • Jerome builds the simplest version that can test the main idea.
  • At this stage, appearance is less important than testing whether the interaction actually works.
  • He tries to prototype one behavior at a time instead of building the entire system immediately.
5. Testing
  • Jerome tests the prototype himself first.
  • Then he lets other people try it without explaining too much.
  • He observes:
  • What they understand immediately
  • Where they hesitate
  • What they do differently from what he expected
  • Unexpected user behavior often becomes part of the design process.
6. Iteration
  • Jerome changes the interaction based on what he learned from testing.
  • Sometimes this means changing the code or hardware.
  • Other times it means simplifying the interaction itself.
  • He usually goes through several small cycles of build → test → adjust.
7. Form & Experience
  • Once the interaction works, Jerome starts thinking more about the overall experience.
  • He refines the physical form, materials, sound, visual feedback, and timing.
  • He tries to make these elements feel like parts of one system rather than separate effects.
8. Finalizing
  • Jerome makes the system more stable and cleans up the physical prototype.
  • He tests the complete experience from beginning to end.
  • He documents the project with photos, videos, diagrams, and notes.
  • He also reflects on what worked, what did not, and what he would change in the next version.
